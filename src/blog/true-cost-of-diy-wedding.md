Every couple I meet has at least paused on the same question: *how much of this can we do ourselves?* It's a fair thing to ask. Weddings aren't cheap, and DIY promises a kind of noble savings — you keep the money, you get the wedding, everyone wins.

Except the receipt isn't the whole cost. Some of the biggest expenses of a DIY wedding never appear on a spreadsheet at all, and by the time you notice them, they've already been paid.

Here's the honest version.

## What DIY actually saves you

Real dollars. There's no denying it. If you skip a planner, build your own timeline, hunt down your own vendors, source your own décor, and coordinate everything on the day, you can genuinely trim thousands off the topline.

For some couples — small guest counts, an all-inclusive venue, a naturally low-stress temperament — that saving is real and worth it. I've told couples that outright. Not everyone needs the level of support I offer, and pretending otherwise would be dishonest.

But before you commit to the DIY path, it's worth understanding what else the invoice includes.

## The costs that don't show up on the spreadsheet

**Your time — hundreds of hours of it.** A DIY wedding is a part-time job for one or both of you for roughly six months. Every hour you spend chasing quotes, comparing linens, or building a timeline is an hour you're not spending doing anything else. Time isn't free just because no one billed you for it.

**Do-over spending.** The chair-cover order that came in the wrong color. The DIY favors that didn't quite turn out. The centerpieces you had to re-do the week of because the tutorial lied about how long they'd stay fresh. Most DIY couples end up buying things twice, and the second purchase is always at the more expensive last-minute price.

**Stress that lands on someone.** DIY doesn't reduce the stress of a wedding — it just relocates it. From a professional to you, or to your mother, or to your maid of honor who now can't relax at the ceremony because she's the one texting the caterer. That stress has a cost even when no one puts a dollar figure on it.

**The wedding you didn't have.** Maybe the biggest hidden cost: the version of the day you would have had if you weren't the one running it. More on that in a minute.

## The math no one shows you

Here's a rough exercise worth doing before you commit to a fully DIY approach: add up your realistic hourly rate — what your time is worth in your normal life — times the ~200 hours a DIY wedding takes. Then add a modest ~10% do-over budget on top of your décor and rentals. Now compare that number to what a coordinator actually costs.

Most couples I've walked through this end up genuinely surprised. The gap between "DIY" and "hire someone" is much narrower than it looks on paper, and once you factor in what you're getting back — your evenings, your weekends, your relationship — the math usually flips.

## When DIY is actually the right call

None of this means you shouldn't do anything yourself. Some of the most beautiful weddings I've been part of had personal, hand-made touches everywhere. Escort cards a couple lettered themselves. A signature drink the groom perfected. Favors packaged one at a time by the couple's parents at the kitchen table, because they wanted to.

The difference is *choosing* the pieces you DIY because they'll mean something — versus DIY-ing the whole day itself and hoping no one drops the ball.

## The middle path most DIY couples end up wishing they'd taken

Here's the honest recommendation I give couples who love the DIY spirit but don't want to pay the hidden costs: **do the planning yourselves, and hire a day-of coordinator for the actual day.**

You keep the parts of DIY that are genuinely fun and genuinely save money — choosing your vendors, designing your day, adding your personal touches. What you hand off is the piece where DIY quietly hurts you the most: the day itself. Someone else builds the final timeline, confirms every vendor, runs your rehearsal, and quietly holds it all together while you actually get married.

It's the smallest possible professional investment, and it protects everything else you've already invested — the money, the hours, the vision.
