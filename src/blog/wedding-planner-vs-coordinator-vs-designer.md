If you have spent an evening searching for help with your wedding, you have probably noticed that the same job goes by many names. Planner, coordinator, designer, day-of, month-of, full-service. Some people use the words interchangeably. Others use them very precisely. It is no wonder couples ask me what the difference is.

I will give you my honest answer, with one caveat up front. There is no official standard. These titles are not regulated, so one professional's "coordinator" may do what another calls "planning." What matters is not the label but what is included, so use this guide to ask better questions.

## What does a wedding planner do?

A planner helps you plan. The work happens over weeks or months, and it is mostly about decisions and organization: understanding what you want, helping you set priorities, suggesting vendors, keeping a checklist moving and keeping you on schedule.

Depending on the service, planning can mean:

- **Full-service planning,** where the planner handles most of the work from the first consultation through the wedding.
- **Partial planning,** where you handle some decisions and the planner supports others, with regular check-ins.

You can see both ends in my packages. My [Unforgettable package](/services/#pkg-unforgettable) is full-service planning, and my [Adoration package](/services/#pkg-adoration) is partial planning combined with coordination on the day.

## What does a wedding coordinator do?

A coordinator makes sure the plan actually happens. The focus is on logistics: the timeline, the vendors, the people and the many small moments that need to land in the right order on the day.

A good coordinator typically:

- Builds a detailed timeline for the wedding day and shares it with everyone.
- Confirms details with vendors in the weeks before.
- Reviews vendor agreements for logistical details.
- Runs the rehearsal.
- Manages setup, vendors, the ceremony, the reception and the wrap-up on the day.

If a planner is the person who helps you decide, a coordinator is the person who makes sure what you decided happens on time. My [Endearment package](/services/#pkg-endearment) is day-of coordination, and it still starts earlier than the wedding, because a good day-of depends on the work done before it. For a closer comparison, I wrote about [day-of coordination versus full planning](/blog/day-of-coordination-vs-full-planning/).

## What does a wedding designer do?

A designer works on how the day looks and feels. That can include the overall style and color story, the layout of the ceremony and reception, florals, tablescapes, lighting and decor.

Some designers work only on design, sometimes alongside a planner. Some planners include design guidance as part of their work. In my services, design and layout help appears in several packages, so look at each one on the [services page](/services/) to see how. If you want a heavily custom look, such as installations or extensive decor, ask any professional how that work is handled and by whom, since it often needs its own scope.

## Where do these roles overlap?

Quite a lot. A planner often coordinates, a coordinator often advises on design, and a designer often needs someone to handle logistics. In a smaller team or a smaller wedding, one person often covers all three at different points.

Here is a simple way to picture it:

- **Planning** is about decisions before the wedding.
- **Design** is about the look of the day.
- **Coordination** is about making the day run.

The overlap is why the question to ask is not "are you a planner or a coordinator?" but "what will you do, and when, and what will I still be responsible for?"

## What about the venue coordinator and other helpers?

Many venues have an on-site coordinator or event manager. That person is a real help, but their job is the venue: the spaces, the rules, the schedule for the building and often the in-house vendors. They do not usually manage your outside vendors or your family or look after your full day.

Other people in the mix:

- **A friend or relative who offers to help.** Generous and often well-meant, but a lot to ask of someone who is also a guest. I wrote about [whether to ask a friend to coordinate your wedding](/blog/friend-coordinate-your-wedding/).
- **Vendor teams.** A photographer or caterer has a team, but they are responsible for their own work, not the whole day.
- **You.** Doing it yourself is possible, and the question is what it costs you in time and attention. My post on [the timeline trap in DIY weddings](/blog/diy-wedding-timeline-trap/) goes into that.

## Which one do I need?

Begin with where you want help, not with the title.

- **Do you want guidance from the beginning,** with someone to help you choose vendors, build a checklist and keep you on track? You are looking for planning support.
- **Do you have the plan, and want someone to run it?** That is coordination.
- **Is the look of the day the part you want help with?** Ask about design and layout guidance, and who handles the hands-on decor.
- **Do you want to hand off nearly everything?** Full-service planning is built for that.

A few signs that you might want more than you think:

- You are planning a wedding with many vendors or moving parts.
- Your date is a few months away, and the list still feels long.
- You expect to be a host at your own wedding, not a manager.
- Family dynamics or travel make the day trickier to run.

And a few signs that a lighter service may be enough:

- You like the planning and want help only on the day.
- Your guest list is small and your plan is simple. You can read about that in [what a micro wedding is](/blog/what-is-a-micro-wedding/).

My post on [whether you really need a wedding coordinator](/blog/do-you-need-a-wedding-coordinator/) is a more honest look at the question of whether to hire anyone at all.

## When should I hire help?

Sooner than most couples expect, although a coordinator can help at many stages. Planning support is most useful before you have signed with vendors, since a planner can advise before the decisions are made. Day-of coordination usually begins well before the wedding. I put the timeline in [when to book your wedding coordinator](/blog/when-to-book-a-wedding-coordinator/).

## What should I ask before I hire anyone?

Whatever the title, ask the same questions:

1. **What exactly is included, and what is not?**
2. **When does your work begin, and when does it end?**
3. **How many hours do you and your team spend on site on the day?** Is there an assistant?
4. **Do you review vendor contracts, and attend walkthroughs or the rehearsal?**
5. **How will we communicate, and how quickly do you respond?**
6. **What happens if something goes wrong on the day, and who decides?**
7. **What are the limits of your service?** For example, a heavy decor setup or travel outside the Portland area may be handled differently.

A professional who answers these clearly, and puts the answers in writing, is usually one worth talking to.

## A short summary

- **Planner:** helps you plan, before the wedding.
- **Coordinator:** runs the logistics, especially on the day.
- **Designer:** shapes the look of the day.
- **Venue coordinator:** looks after the venue, not your whole wedding.
- **The right choice:** the one that covers the part you least want to carry yourself.

Titles are only a starting point. Ask what is included, ask who does it and ask when, and the right answer will usually become clear.
