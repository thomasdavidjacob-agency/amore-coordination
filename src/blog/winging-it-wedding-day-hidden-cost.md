Every once in a while, a couple tells me confidently that they've decided to just "wing" their wedding day. No coordinator, no strict timeline, no vendor point person. *"We're pretty chill,"* they say. *"We just want it to feel natural."*

I always want to write them a letter — one they'd read only on the morning after — because I've seen how this story tends to end. It's not that anything goes catastrophically wrong. It's that "winging it" turns out to have a cost most couples never priced in until it was already spent.

## What "winging it" actually costs

The savings on paper are real: no coordinator invoice. What isn't on paper is what happens instead.

Because someone still has to make every small decision that comes up on a wedding day, and there are dozens of them. When there's no coordinator, that someone is you. In a wedding dress. Holding a bouquet. Trying to be present.

- The florist calls to ask which door is the freight door.
- The caterer needs to know whether to hold service because hair ran long.
- Your mom is asking if she's supposed to be seated yet.
- The DJ is asking who's giving the first toast.
- Your officiant wants to confirm which reading is happening in what order.
- The photographer wants your group-shot list because you never sent one.
- Someone knocked over a candle.
- The venue coordinator (who is not a wedding coordinator — that's a different job) is asking about the bar tab structure.
- Your cousin is asking where to put the gift he brought.

Every one of those is a five-second question. There are fifty of them across a wedding day. And each one is a small pull on your attention that's supposed to be pointed at the person you're marrying.

## What "free" really costs

Here's what most couples don't realize until it's too late: winging it doesn't save you the coordinator's fee. It just **converts that fee into your own labor** — priced at the worst possible hourly rate, because you're doing it in your wedding attire during the one day of your life you can't get back.

The costs it converts into:

- **Your presence.** You'll spend chunks of the day mentally managing rather than being in it. Your partner will notice. Your best photos will subtly show it.
- **Your family's presence.** If it's not you, it's your mom, or your maid of honor, or your father-in-law, doing the running. They came to celebrate you. They ended up working.
- **The pace of the day.** With no one actively holding the timeline, things slip. Guests notice. You'll feel behind for the last three hours of the reception even though you can't explain why.
- **The memory itself.** Ask any couple who winged it what they remember most about the middle of their reception. Most of them will laugh nervously and say "honestly, it's kind of a blur — I was running around a lot."

## What it feels like when someone else holds the day

The couples I've worked with describe it almost identically afterward. They talk about **not knowing what time it was**, in a good way. About realizing at some point that they'd stopped checking their phone. About their mother finally sitting down to eat because no one was asking her anything. About standing on the dance floor at 10pm looking at each other and thinking, *this was actually the day we imagined.*

That's what you're really paying a coordinator for. Not for a timeline document. Not for vendor emails. For the very specific gift of being a **guest at your own wedding.**

## The smallest professional investment in the whole industry

Here's the honest truth: if the reason you're winging it is the money, a **day-of coordinator** is almost certainly cheaper than what you're already planning to spend on flowers or the bar tab. It's the smallest professional line item in the whole wedding, and the one that protects every other dollar you've already committed.

You keep your DIY savings on everything else. You just make sure you're not the person answering "should we push cake by 15 minutes?" while trying to eat your dinner.
