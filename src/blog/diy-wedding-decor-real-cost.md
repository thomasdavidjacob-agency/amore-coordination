DIY décor is where most couples first fall in love with the idea of doing their wedding themselves. It's easy to see why. Pinterest is full of beautiful, personal, hand-crafted setups that look like they cost a fraction of the professional version. The couple in the photo looks so proud. You look at the caption — "we made everything ourselves!" — and think, *we could do this.*

You probably could. What almost none of those posts mention is what it actually cost them.

## The math that gets left out

Let's take one very common DIY project — 20 wooden-crate-and-mason-jar centerpieces with fresh eucalyptus and candles. Here's roughly what it actually adds up to:

- **Raw materials:** wooden crates, mason jars, twine, tea lights, candle holders, floral foam, water tubes, chalk labels. Realistically ~$18–25 per centerpiece if you're careful. That's $360–500 before you touch flowers.
- **Flowers:** wholesale eucalyptus, filler greenery, and a few blooms per jar — $8–15 per centerpiece bought Costco/wholesale. Add $160–300.
- **Tools:** hot glue gun, wire cutters, floral shears, spray sealant, staple gun. Maybe you own some of these. Maybe not. $40–80.
- **Test batches:** almost everyone makes 2–3 practice centerpieces to figure out the design. Add ~$50.
- **The one thing that doesn't turn out**, so you redo it late. Another $75.

You're already at **$685–1,010** — and we haven't talked about your time, or how you're getting them to the venue, or what happens to them at 11pm when everyone leaves.

Compare that to what a modest florist would charge for 20 centerpieces: often in the same range, sometimes less, and *they* deal with the flowers, the setup, the water, the teardown, and the fact that eucalyptus starts curling if it sits out too long.

## The costs that aren't materials

Even when DIY décor genuinely saves you money on paper, it comes with a set of costs no one puts on the receipt:

**Time.** Every serious DIY décor project I've watched a couple take on has swallowed 40–80 hours of their lives. Weekends spent gluing, evenings spent shopping for the right shade of ribbon, the Saturday you'd meant to relax completely eaten by centerpieces. That time is not free. Ask what else you would have done with those weekends.

**Storage.** You need somewhere to put 20 completed centerpieces for weeks before the wedding, plus the linens, the signage, the favors, the arch materials. Small apartments become obstacle courses. Living rooms become staging warehouses. This has a cost too.

**Transport.** Someone has to get all of it to the venue. That's usually a rented SUV, a couple of trips, and a friend recruited to help load and unload — often on the morning of the wedding, when you should be getting your hair done.

**Setup labor.** Someone has to actually *place* everything at the venue. Twenty centerpieces don't set themselves. Signage doesn't hang itself. This alone is 3–5 hours of work on the wedding day, done by someone — often the couple, or the couple's parents, in their nice clothes.

**Teardown.** At the end of the night, everything you brought has to leave with someone. This is the part every DIY couple forgets. At 10:45pm, exhausted, still in wedding attire, you or someone you love is loading 20 dusty centerpieces back into an SUV in the parking lot.

**Risk.** Things break. Rain happens. Candles don't stay lit. The garland you spent 15 hours on droops in the humidity. There's no professional insurance on your work; there's just you, standing in front of it at 4pm, deciding what to do.

## What DIY décor IS actually great for

I want to be clear — DIY absolutely has a place, and some of the most beautiful weddings I've been part of had personal, hand-made touches everywhere. The difference is in **which** things you DIY.

DIY works beautifully for:

- **A single meaningful piece** — a hand-lettered welcome sign, custom vow books, a signature drink recipe from the two of you, a family recipe served at cocktail hour.
- **Favors,** which can be assembled over months and don't need to survive setup or weather.
- **Personal touches** at each place setting — a note, a photo, a small object with meaning.

Where DIY tends to hurt couples is when it becomes the *entire décor strategy* — because then all those setup, transport, and teardown costs land on someone on the wedding day, and that someone is almost always you or your family.

## Where a day-of coordinator quietly saves this

Here's what most DIY-décor couples eventually discover the hard way: even if you buy or build every piece yourself, someone still has to **manage it on the day.** Someone has to set it out at the venue, oversee the layout, adjust when something isn't quite right, and box it back up at the end of the night.

A day-of coordinator does all of that. You keep the money you saved by sourcing and creating your décor yourself. You just hand off the part that would otherwise ruin your morning, your evening, or your parents' Sunday. That's a very different math than "hire a full planner," and it's usually the sweet spot for DIY-inclined couples.
