A venue is the first big decision in a holiday party, and it quietly makes a dozen other decisions for you. It sets the capacity, the layout, the rules on food and drink, how late you can stay and when your vendors can arrive. Choose well and the rest of the party gets easier. Choose on looks alone and you can end up renegotiating everything.

This is the list of questions I use when I sit down with a client to compare rooms. It sits under my broader guide to [holiday party planning in Portland](/blog/holiday-party-planning-portland-coordinators-guide/), and it works for a company party, a client event or a large private celebration.

## What do I decide before I start touring?

Three things, so that every venue answers the same questions:

- **Headcount range.** A realistic minimum and maximum.
- **Format.** Seated dinner, cocktail-style reception, buffet, stations, or a mix.
- **Two or three acceptable dates, including a weeknight.** Flexibility often unlocks a better room.

If time is short, my guide to [last-minute holiday party planning](/blog/last-minute-holiday-party-planning/) shows how to narrow these quickly.

I will not name specific Portland venues here. Rules, availability and offerings change, and the only reliable source is the venue itself. What I can give you is the list of questions that separates a venue that fits from one that only photographs well.

## What is the real capacity for my format?

A venue may advertise one number, but capacity depends on how you use the room. A space that holds a crowd standing can feel cramped once you add tables, a bar, a buffet line and a place for coats.

Ask for capacity in the format you want, not just the maximum. Then ask to see the floor plan or to walk the room with your layout in mind. Where will the bar go? Where will people queue? Where does a speaker stand so the whole room can hear?

## What is included, and what do I have to bring?

Two venues with the same rental fee can be very different once you add what each one lacks. Ask specifically about:

- Tables, chairs and linens, and whether the quantity matches your headcount.
- Glassware, plates and flatware, or whether the caterer supplies them.
- Heating, cooling and lighting, which matter in a winter room.
- Sound: a built-in system, a microphone for toasts, or nothing.
- Staff on the day: a venue manager, a custodian, anyone who will be there for the full event.
- Setup and breakdown: whether the venue handles resetting the room or leaves it to you.

Write the answers down in the same format for each venue so you can compare them side by side.

## What are the food and drink rules?

This is where surprises live. Ask:

- Is there an in-house caterer, a required list, or can I bring my own?
- Can I bring in desserts, a specialty vendor or a cake from elsewhere?
- What are the rules on alcohol: who may serve it, whether a license is needed and who is responsible for it?
- Are there limits on candles, open flame, confetti, or decor that is attached to walls?

I do not state what the rules are, because they differ by venue, and in some cases by who is serving. Ask the venue to explain, and ask your caterer to confirm their side. If either hesitates, that is worth knowing before you sign.

## How late can we stay, and when can vendors load in?

Time limits are as important as the headcount. Ask for the exact access window: when the venue opens to your vendors, when guests may arrive, when the party must end and when everyone must be out.

Then count backward. A party that runs until a certain hour usually requires vendors to be finishing before that hour. Allow time for breakdown, return of rentals and a final walk-through. If the contract gives you only the party hours, you may not have enough time to set up.

## What is the plan for parking, access and arrival?

In December, a venue's convenience matters as much as its charm. Guests arriving in the rain, in the dark, often after a day of work, are less patient with confusing parking.

Ask:

- Where do guests park, and how far is the walk?
- Is there a covered or close drop-off?
- Is the venue accessible for guests who need step-free entry, and where is the accessible route?
- Where do vendors unload, and is there a loading area that does not block guests?
- Is there a coat check area or space for one?

Local weather is a reason to be thorough. The National Weather Service's [Climate of Portland](https://www.weather.gov/media/pqr/climate/ClimateBookPortland/PDXclimatebook.pdf) book, using 1991 to 2020 normals, lists November and December as the two wettest months in Portland. A good arrival plan is part of the venue, not an afterthought.

## What if I want an outdoor or partly outdoor space?

Plan as I would for a wedding. If the party includes a patio, courtyard or garden, ask what the covered or indoor alternative is and who decides when to use it. The same logic runs through my [Oregon outdoor wedding rain backup plan](/blog/oregon-outdoor-wedding-rain-backup-plan/): the second option should be one you would be happy with, with the decision made before the day.

## Can I visit when the room is set up?

If you can, do. A room at rest tells you little about the room at a party. Ask if you can see it during a similar event, or at least at the time of day your party will happen. Check the lighting at dusk, how loud the room is with people in it, and how the flow works from the door to the bar to the seating.

## Do I still need my own coordinator if the venue has one?

Usually the roles are different. A venue coordinator knows the building, its rules and its staff, and makes sure the space is ready. A coordinator who works for you is responsible for your vendors, your timeline, your decor and your guests' experience.

I wrote about the same split for weddings, in [day-of coordination vs. full planning](/blog/day-of-coordination-vs-full-planning/). The principle is the same: someone needs to own the whole event, not just the space.

## A short checklist to bring to every tour

- Capacity for my format, not just the maximum.
- Layout: bar, buffet, seating, speaker position, coat check.
- Included items, and what I must rent.
- Catering and alcohol rules.
- Access window for vendors, party hours and out-by time.
- Parking, drop-off and accessible entry.
- Rain or cold-weather plan.
- Who on the venue side is present on the day, and who is my point of contact.

Ask the same questions of every venue, and the right room tends to make itself obvious.
